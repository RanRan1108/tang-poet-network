# Digital Humanities Project: Tang Dynasty Poets' Social Network and Geographic Visualization

## Project Overview
This project applies digital humanities methods to organize and visualize the social relationships and geographical distribution of major Tang Dynasty poets. By constructing a poet social network, mapping birthplaces, and tracing individual travel routes, it explores the interaction patterns and spatial mobility of literary groups in the Tang Dynasty.

## Tools and Technologies
- **Programming Language**: Python 3.13
- **Data Processing**: Pandas
- **Network Analysis**: NetworkX
- **Visualization**: Matplotlib
- **GIS Tools**: Lantu Map (兰图绘)
- **Version Control**: Git & Gitee

## Project Contents
### 1. Tang Poet Social Network Graph
Built a dataset of poet interactions based on historical records. Used NetworkX to calculate centrality, revealing the core social status of poets such as Li Bai and Du Fu.

### 2. Birthplace Distribution Map
Collected longitude and latitude data of major poets' birthplaces. Created scatter plots to show the geographical distribution. Key finding: Li Bai was born in Suyab (in modern-day Kyrgyzstan), geographically distant from the cultural center of the Central Plains, yet he occupied a central position in the social network.

### 3. Interactive Birthplace Map
Used Lantu Map to create an interactive online map showing poet birthplaces.

### 4. Li Bai's Travel Route Map
Organized important locations of Li Bai's life in chronological order, visualizing his legendary journey from birth to death.

### 5. Du Fu's Travel Route Map
Created a parallel map for Du Fu, showing his life of displacement and wandering.

### 6. Li Bai and Du Fu Route Comparison
Combined both routes on a single map for direct comparison, revealing the contrast between Li Bai's free-spirited wandering and Du Fu's turbulent displacement.

## Key Findings
Li Bai's geographical marginality (born in Suyab) contrasts sharply with his social centrality. Geographically a "marginal man," he was nevertheless the "central node" of the Tang poetry world.
